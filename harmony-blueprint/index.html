Why It's Different

Harmony Blueprint creates harmonic tension.

Staying in the key is safe, but modern music is built on harmonic tension — the pull between your notes and the chord underneath them. Snap to scale only knows the key, so it can't create that. Harmony Blueprint snaps your melody to each chord as it changes, across the entire keyboard — so wherever you play, your notes land against the harmony.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

How is this different from snap to scale?

Snap to scale locks your notes to a key — one fixed set of notes for the whole song. That keeps you in the key, but it can't create harmonic tension, the foundation of modern music, because it doesn't know which chord is playing. Harmony Blueprint follows your chord progression and moves with it as the chords change. And because every chord is mapped across the entire keyboard, it works in every octave, from bass to lead.

Does this work with my DAW?

Yes — it runs in any DAW that supports VST3 or AU on Mac and Windows, and it's tested and proven in FL Studio, Ableton, Logic and Studio One. If you hit a snag in another DAW, message me and I'll help.

Can I use my own plugins or instruments with Harmony Blueprint?

Yes — just route Harmony Blueprint's MIDI output into your instrument's MIDI input, and you'll hear your own sound instead of Harmony Blueprint's. You're not limited to what's built in — the possibilities are basically endless.

Can I use the chords from my own song?

Yes — drag a MIDI file onto the plugin and it builds the chord map from your file, press Learn and play your chords on your keyboard, or click them in by hand. Up to 8 chords, and they all snap your melody just like the built-in progressions.

Do I need to know music theory to use this?

No — that's the whole point. You'll start understanding it just by using the tool.

Can I use these commercially?

Yes — every progression is 100% royalty-free with unlimited commercial use. No limits, no additional clearance needed. (The only restriction: you can't resell the MIDI patterns/maps themselves as a standalone product.)
